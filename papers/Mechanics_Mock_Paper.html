Instructions

1. [10 marks]
A car is travelling along a straight horizontal road. At time t = 0 the car passes a point A with speed 25 m s−1. The car accelerates uniformly for 8 s until it reaches a speed of 45 m s−1. It then travels at this constant speed for a further 20 s before applying the brakes and decelerating uniformly to rest at point B.
(a) Sketch a velocity–time graph for the motion of the car from A to B, clearly labelling the times and velocities at each stage. [2]
(b) Find the total time taken for the car to travel from A to B. [2]
(c) Find the total distance travelled from A to B. [3]
(d) Find the deceleration of the car during the final stage of the motion, given that the distance travelled during deceleration is 180 m. [3]
2. [8 marks]
A particle P of mass 4 kg lies on a rough horizontal table and is attached to one end of a light inextensible string which passes over a small smooth pulley fixed at the edge of the table. The other end of the string is attached to a particle Q of mass 3 kg which hangs vertically below the pulley, as shown in the diagram. The system is released from rest with the string taut. The coefficient of friction between particle P and the table is μ. The tension in the string is 24.5 N.
(a) By considering particle Q, show that the acceleration of the system is 1.63 m s−2 (to 3 s.f.). [3]
(b) Find the value of μ. [5]
3. [12 marks]
A lift of mass 500 kg is descending vertically. The lift is supported by a light vertical cable. Inside the lift, a box of mass 40 kg rests on the floor.
(a) Draw separate force diagrams showing all the forces acting on (i) the lift, and (ii) the box. [3]
(b) Given that the tension in the cable is 4200 N, find the magnitude of the acceleration of the lift. [3]
(c) Hence find the magnitude of the force exerted by the floor of the lift on the box. [3]
(d) A person of mass 70 kg now also stands in the lift on top of the box. By considering the system as a whole, find the new tension in the cable needed to produce the same downward acceleration found in part (b). [3]
4. [10 marks]
A particle of mass 6 kg is projected with speed 18 m s−1 directly up a rough plane inclined at 30° to the horizontal. The coefficient of friction between the particle and the plane is 0.25.
(a) Find the magnitude of the frictional force acting on the particle. [3]
(b) By resolving parallel to the plane, find the deceleration of the particle. [3]
(c) Show that the particle comes to rest after travelling approximately 14.7 m up the plane. [3]
(d) State, with a reason, whether the particle will begin to slide back down the plane once it comes to rest. [1]
5. [11 marks]
Two spheres A and B of masses 5 kg and 3 kg respectively are moving towards each other along the same straight line on a smooth horizontal surface. Sphere A has speed 4 m s−1 and sphere B has speed 2 m s−1. After the collision sphere B moves in the same direction as A was originally moving, with speed 3 m s−1.
(a) Find the speed and direction of sphere A after the collision. [4]
(b) Find the coefficient of restitution e for the collision. [3]
(c) Calculate the loss of kinetic energy in the collision. [4]
6. [11 marks]
A particle is projected horizontally from the top of a vertical cliff of height 78.4 m above the sea with a speed of 24.5 m s−1, as shown in the diagram below.
(a) Find the time taken for the particle to reach the sea. [2]
(b) Find the horizontal distance from the base of the cliff to the point where the particle hits the sea. [2]
(c) Find the speed and direction of motion of the particle when it reaches the sea. [4]
(d) A second particle is projected from the same point at the same speed 24.5 m s−1, but at an angle of 30° above the horizontal. Find the horizontal distance travelled by this particle before it hits the sea. [3]
7. [13 marks]
A body of mass 8 kg moves in a straight line across a rough horizontal surface. At point A the speed of the body is 12 m s−1. A constant horizontal force of magnitude 30 N acts on the body in the direction of motion. As the body moves from A to B, a distance of 20 m, the speed of the body increases to 14 m s−1.
(a) Using the work–energy principle, or otherwise, find the magnitude of the work done against the resistances to motion as the body moves from A to B. [4]
(b) Hence find the magnitude of the resistive force, assuming it is constant. [2]
A second body of mass 12 kg is projected from B with speed 5 m s−1 up a smooth plane inclined at an angle θ to the horizontal, where sin θ = 1/7. The same constant force of 30 N now acts on the body, but directed down the plane.
(c) Find the acceleration of the body. [4]
(d) The body comes to instantaneous rest after travelling a distance d metres from B. Find the value of d. [3]
8. [10 marks]
A uniform rod AB of mass 8 kg and length 3 m rests horizontally in equilibrium supported at two points C and D, where AC = 0.5 m and AD = 2.5 m. A particle of mass 4 kg is placed on the rod at B.
(a) By taking moments about C, find the magnitude of the reaction force at D. [4]
(b) Hence find the magnitude of the reaction force at C. [2]
The particle of mass 4 kg is now moved to a new position X on the rod.
(c) Given that the reaction force at C is equal to the reaction force at D, find the distance AX. [4]
